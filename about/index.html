ABOUT

Building enterprises.
Building systems.
Building ecosystems.

Raksha Thammaiah is a strategy, innovation and venture-building professional whose work has consistently focused on one question: how do you take an idea, business or initiative and build the systems around it to make it viable, operational and capable of growing?

From engineering to enterprise

Raksha's foundation is in engineering and engineering management. She holds a Bachelor's degree in Information Science & Engineering from Coorg Institute of Technology and an M.Tech in Engineering Management from R.V. College of Engineering, Bengaluru.

Her work at RVCE moved towards innovation and intellectual property, including research into patenting practices, IP culture, valuation and research commercialisation. She later completed WIPO programmes in intellectual property, including Advanced Intellectual Property Management.

Building businesses and systems

Entrepreneurship has been a recurring part of Raksha's career. She co-founded Uddip Consultancy Services, working across strategy, innovation, intellectual property, go-to-market, operational systems and business transformation. Today, that founder perspective also extends to FarmtoCups, a consumer beverage venture rooted in coffee, origin, direct sourcing and product innovation.

At Helpful Engineering, an international volunteer-driven engineering organisation, her work evolved across strategy and operations leadership, including organisational structures, operating processes, programmes and innovation systems. Experience with LetsVenture added a venture and investment ecosystem perspective.

Innovation, research and commercialisation

Raksha's published work has covered patenting culture in Indian educational institutions, intellectual-property valuation, VR and IoT-enabled learning environments, and e-waste management. Her interests include technology policy, research commercialisation, innovation strategy, economic development and open innovation.

Teaching, mentoring and ecosystems

Alongside her primary professional work, Raksha has worked with students, professionals and founders across business communication, entrepreneurship, design thinking, business models, pitching and decision-making. Her mentoring experience includes the MIT COVID-19 Challenge and Kerala Startup Mission, including MIND engagements with early-revenue and growth-stage startups.

Her ecosystem work includes CII–Young Indians Mysuru and initiatives such as Mysuru Entrepreneurship Habba, connecting founders, investors, institutions and other participants in the entrepreneurial ecosystem.

Different sides of the same table

Raksha has been a researcher studying intellectual property and innovation; a founder building businesses; a consultant working with entrepreneurs and organisations; an operator translating strategy into systems; a professional exposed to the venture ecosystem; an educator and mentor; and an ecosystem builder.

That range has shaped a simple belief: business problems rarely exist in isolation. A go-to-market problem may actually be a product problem. A growth problem may be an operating-system problem. An investment-readiness problem may begin with governance or unit economics. An innovation problem may have less to do with generating ideas than with creating the structures required to commercialise them.

Her work ultimately sits at the intersection of building enterprises, building systems and building ecosystems.
