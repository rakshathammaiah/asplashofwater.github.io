ABOUT RAKSHA

Different sides of
the same table.

A career across engineering, research, entrepreneurship, consulting, operations, venture ecosystems, mentoring and community building.

I have rarely stayed on only one side of a business problem. I have built, advised, operated, researched, evaluated, taught and mentored. That movement is what shaped the way I work today.

The foundation: engineering, research and intellectual property

My academic foundation began in Information Science & Engineering and continued with an M.Tech in Engineering Management at R.V. College of Engineering, Bengaluru. During this period my interests moved beyond technology itself towards the systems that allow technology and research to create value.

My research explored intellectual property, patent orientation, valuation and innovation in technical institutions. I have four publications spanning intellectual-property valuation, patenting and innovation, technology-enabled learning and e-waste. I also completed WIPO learning in intellectual property. Research taught me to question assumptions and to separate an interesting idea from evidence that it can work.

Teaching before consulting became the career

Alongside the early stages of my professional journey, I spent four semesters between 2014 and 2016 teaching credit courses in business communication to engineering students at Malnad College of Engineering through an L&D organisation. That experience became an early introduction to facilitation: taking concepts that appear obvious to professionals and making them useful to people encountering them for the first time.

Entrepreneurship and Uddip

I went on to co-found Uddip Consultancy Services, a boutique strategy and innovation consulting firm. The work has taken me across technology and product-driven startups and SMEs, supporting enterprise creation, business strategy, intellectual-property management, go-to-market, operational excellence, systems and business transformation, investment readiness and innovation management.

Across Uddip and related work, I have supported businesses at different points in their lifecycle—from an early founder trying to make the business model coherent to an established SME trying to formalise systems, build new products or prepare for the next phase of growth.

Seeing the company from the venture side

At LetsVenture, I worked across startup and venture programmes and gained a different view of businesses: from the side of capital. My work included running investment committee processes and contributing to the lifecycle design of a ₹1,100 crore venture fund. Working with more than 200 startups across LetsVenture and Uddip sharpened the distinction between a compelling story and a business that has the structure to support that story.

Operating inside an international organisation

Helpful Engineering gave me another perspective. I moved through strategy and operations leadership roles—from team lead to Director and Deputy COO—working on organisational systems, partnerships, programmes and innovation frameworks in a distributed international engineering community.

The work reinforced something I had already begun to see in consulting: strategy becomes useful only when people, decisions, responsibilities and operating systems make it executable.

Building again: FarmtoCups

FarmtoCups brought me back to the founder's side of the table. Rooted in our connection to coffee estates in Coorg, the venture has involved direct sourcing, product development, pricing, packaging, channels, customer behaviour, experiences and the everyday decisions behind a consumer business.

It is also where many threads in my career meet—systems thinking, value chains, product innovation, traditional knowledge, consumer behaviour and the discipline of actually building what one might otherwise advise another founder to build.

Mentoring, ecosystems and Mysuru

I have continued to work with founders as a mentor, including through Kerala Startup Mission and earlier through the MIT COVID-19 Challenge. My ecosystem work has included CII–Young Indians Mysuru, Breakfast with Founders and initiatives such as Mysuru Entrepreneurship Habba, where the work shifts from supporting an individual enterprise to asking what a city or community needs for more enterprises to emerge and grow.

Sport is part of the architecture too

Before much of this, there was swimming. I trained professionally for around 15 years and competed at national level. Endurance sport has remained part of my life, including long-distance running and the Tata Mumbai Marathon Legends Club. Sport gave me an unusually early education in repetition, marginal improvement, recovery, competition and staying with a process long after the excitement of starting has disappeared.

The work today

Today, my work sits at the intersection of building enterprises, building systems and building ecosystems. I am particularly interested in problems where strategy cannot be separated from execution: innovation and commercialisation, GTM, operating systems, venture building, research-to-market, founder decision-making and entrepreneurship ecosystems.

I also speak and facilitate sessions around these subjects, bringing together examples from businesses I have worked with, businesses I have built, research, sport and the questions that emerge from sitting on different sides of the same table.
